Case study · JLR

Synthetic data for vehicle inspection

A photorealistic digital twin of a vehicle inspection tunnel, built in NVIDIA Omniverse, that renders 12,000 auto-annotated images per node per day to train computer vision models before a single real photo is taken.

Overview

Training data without waiting for the cars

Vision models that inspect vehicles need large, labelled image sets. Real images only exist once a vehicle is built, and every label costs time to add by hand.

I led JLR's synthetic data project as its sole developer. I modelled the inspection tunnels as digital twins in Blender, brought vehicles in from production CAD via Deltagen and OpenUSD, and tuned materials and lighting until, to the human eye, the synthetic images could not be told apart from real tunnel captures. The pipeline then rendered and labelled datasets automatically.

The extensions are object-oriented and vehicle-agnostic, so a new vehicle is onboarded by updating its USD prim paths. A current production vehicle serves as a control: it has real tunnel images, so its synthetic data can be checked against reality. One future programme is fully set up and a second is being onboarded.

How it works

From CAD to training-ready dataset

Digital twin

Inspection tunnels modelled in Blender and assembled in Omniverse to match the real geometry, lighting and camera positions.

CAD to USD

Vehicle CAD processed in Deltagen and exported to OpenUSD, so unreleased models could be imaged early.

Automation

Custom Omniverse extensions to move wheels, place vehicles and drive them through the tunnel.

Photorealism

Paint, materials and lighting tuned until synthetic frames matched real tunnel images.

Render & label

Automated rendering with bounding boxes generated alongside each image, exported as COCO JSON, JSONL and CSV, then used to train RF-DETR models.

Outcomes

What it delivered

  • 12,000 annotated images per node per day, generated without anyone needing to take or label a photo.
  • Training data for a future vehicle was ready before it physically existed, with a second programme being onboarded.
  • A current vehicle was used as a control, so synthetic images could be checked against real tunnel images.
  • Models trained with GCP AutoML and an internal RF-DETR pipeline were benchmarked against curated test datasets.
  • To the human eye, the most recent datasets could not be told apart from real tunnel images.
